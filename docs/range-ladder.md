# The range ladder (14 Nov 2026)

*Agreed with the user on 14 Nov 2026, as the first design talk after `docs/pillars.md`. It covers what pushes you from the lander out to the far Reach and down into the deep: the rings, what holds you back, how you get past it, and where the walkers and the ending sit. Distances are from the main base. All numbers are starting points for playtest.*

## The world: 6 × 6 km, and deeper rather than wider

- **The region stays 6 × 6 km** (the user: if we grew it, there would need to be "a damn good reason"). "Further" means **down** as well as out: the surface, then the canyon floor, then cliff caves, then the lava tube, then the deep where the heart shows.
- **Why down suits the story:** life won't be found on the surface. The best geology, water and ice are below too.
- **6 km should feel big because of:**
  - ground that costs speed and air (traversal phase 2);
  - obstacles that need the right kit;
  - a rover slowed to a realistic pace, about 3 m/s instead of today's 6.5.
- The map grows only if playtests say it's too small.
- **The terrain needs a big rework** to carry all this: the obstacles below, the vertical layers and the HiRISE references. It gets its own design doc, replacing much of `docs/terrain-overhaul.md`.

## What holds you back: distance is soft, terrain is hard

There are never locked doors, only Mars.
- **Distance is a soft gate.** Your air, battery and food set how far you can get and still come back, and better gear stretches that. You can always try; Mars decides whether you make it.
- **Terrain is a hard gate.** Some places need a tool or a skill for their ground: ropes for a cliff, a lamp and spare air for a cave, the rover for the dune sea, a bridge span for a fissure.

## Obstacles and how you get past them

All of these are real Mars landforms; the Aeolis area has many of them. They're placed in the ring where they first appear, and the tougher versions come further out.

| Obstacle | What it does | How you get past it | First in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cliffs and steep faces** | the 25° flight rule; scrambling up to 45° | ropes and anchors you print, GAR-Y's winch for short faces, then Castle's waiver (traversal phase 5) | ring 2 |
| **Skylights and pits** | collapse pits in the lava tube's roof | rope down through a skylight to enter the tube on foot, long before the rover can | ring 3 |
| **The dune sea and loose ground** | sand drinks your air and bogs the rover | read the ground; the drone finds a firm line and drops markers; stake trails firm it up; go round by the rock; rover wheel add-ons | ring 3 |
| **Dark and cold places** | caves, the shadowed canyon floor and the tube's cold trap drain your lamp and heater | lamp and battery add-ons, light stakes you leave behind, GAR-Y's lights following you in | ring 2 |
| **Yardang mazes** | wind-carved ridges 10 to 50 m tall, with no straight line through | the drone threads the way and drops markers; once trailed, a maze becomes a shortcut | ring 2 |
| **Ripple belts and bogging** | bands of sand ripples; real rovers got stuck in them (Spirit for good, Opportunity for weeks) | dig out, lay printed traction mats, or GAR-Y's winch pulls you free; on foot they eat air | ring 3 |
| **Sharp boulder fields** | wind-sharpened rocks of the kind that tore holes in Curiosity's wheels | drive through and wear the wheels (spares and repairs), or go round and lose time | ring 3 |
| **Dust devils** | a passing one blinds you briefly and coats your visor, but it can also clean your solar panels | wait it out; their dark tracks mark loose dust | ring 1 |
| **Raised old riverbeds** | ancient channels left standing as ridges of hard gravel | not an obstacle but a **reward route**: a causeway across sand if you read the land, with Life and Climate finds | ring 2 |
| **Buried glaciers** | ice under rubble at the feet of mesas and scarps: rich ice, but full of sublimation pits and hollows | GAR-Y's radar shows the safe ground | ring 2 |
| **Fresh craters with ice** | a meteor strike mid-run makes a new crater, with bright ice that's gone in a few sols | an event: a race to core it before it sublimates | any ring |
| **Dawn frost on slopes** | slippery for boots and wheels until about noon | timing: climb in the afternoon. The frost is also a clue to ice below | ring 1 |
| **Fissures** | long, deep cracks like Cerberus Fossae, too wide to cross on foot | go round to where it narrows, rope across, or print a bridge span for the rover; the fissures lead towards the heart | ring 3 |
| **Marsquakes and rockfalls** | quakes of the kind InSight recorded shake the hab and drop rock in the canyon and the tube, blocking or opening passages | don't linger under cliffs; reroute; they come more often towards the end | ring 2 (rare), then rising |
| **Platy lava fields** | cracked, jagged slabs of young lava, slow on foot and hard on wheels | find the few safe lines (drone, trails); a belt you must cross to reach the tube | ring 3 |
| **Hollow ground** | thin crust over voids near the tube that can collapse into small pits | GAR-Y's radar or a probe pole shows where it's safe; skylights are the big version | ring 3 |
| **Radio shadow** | down in the canyon or a cave, Castle can't hear you (like CHAPEA's blackouts) | place relay beacons on the rim to carry the radio down; the deep stays out of reach, so the last stretch is you alone | ring 2 |
| **Solar storms** | the green aurora warns you, and you have a few hours to get under cover | a cave, the rover or a shielded room; a dosimeter on your wrist | ring 2 onward (events) |
| **Dust storms** | seasonal: visibility drops, solar power falls and landmarks vanish | trails, beacons and batteries carry you through; the big one is the storm's return near the end | events; the finale |
| **Nav pockets** | small pockets where the buried heart throws your compass and fixes off, as it did Walker 1's | navigate by the sun, your stake trails and the drone's markers; the pockets grow nearer the heart, and the story becomes the obstacle | ring 2 (one small pocket), then rising |

**Later, not now:** body and kit hazards (toxic perchlorate dust indoors, dust wearing seals, the cold sapping batteries, long shadows at dawn and dusk). They're ideas to consider after the ladder is built.

## Your gear: modular kit you print

- **The suit starts short and bare:**
  - about a 600 m round trip of air;
  - a dim lamp;
  - no heater pack;
  - no add-ons.
  
  That's enough for the chapters' pallets, one at a time. **This means rewriting the opening hours** (the chapters and their distances) to match.
- **Add-ons go in a few slots:** a bigger air pack, a second battery, a brighter lamp, a heater pack, a sample case and a rope kit. Each one is a found plan plus materials at the Print Shop.
- **What you carry is a decision on every trip.** For example, for the cave you swap the sample case for a spare lamp battery.
- **The robots grow the same way:** GAR-Y's add-ons (deeper radar, a winch, a longer battery, a second arm), the drone's range, and the rover's wheels and traction mats.

## Staying out overnight

- **On foot you're always a day trip** from a base or the lander.
- **The pressurised rover lets you sleep out:** park, eat, sleep, and recharge if the sun's up. For example: drive to the canyon, sleep in the rover, rope down at dawn and be back by the next dusk.
- **Outposts are permanent stops.**
- There's no carried shelter; the rover keeps its pull.

## The walkers: four, so four bases

1. **Walker 1** seeds the main base in the chapters.
2. **Walker 2** is in ring 2.
3. **Walkers 3 and 4** are in ring 3.

Each later walker is lost in its own way: stuck in a dune, down a slump, or wandered off towards the heart. Each one found is a big moment, and a new base wherever you choose.

## The scout drone

- It sits in a cargo wreck at the edge of ring 2, beside the first yardang maze, where it's needed, along with its plan cartridge.
- You learn it by threading the maze. It drops markers that join your stake trails (traversal phase 3).
- Its range and battery grow with add-ons.

## The four goals' trails

Each goal has places in every ring, so every goal pulls you outward. The details are filled in as the narrative is built.

| Goal (owner) | Places |
|---|---|
| **Life** (Tupou) | layered clay, the old lakebed, the cave mineral crusts, the hot-spring trace in the deep |
| **Climate** (Rao) | frost hollows, the ice scarp, the buried glaciers, the tube's cold trap |
| **Geology** (Achterberg) | outcrops, mesa layers, the canyon floor, the fissures |
| **Humans** (Mwangi) | the hab, a cave refuge, the lava tube as a home for the crew |

## The deep and the ending

The last big trip goes down the lava tube to where it cuts the old magnetised body:
- the compass spins;
- the rock is warm from the young pocket below;
- this is where the hot springs were fed.

You place the last magnetometer, the magnetic map completes and the crew's way down is safe.

**You make it, barely.** The climb out is the hardest trip of the game:
- your air is low and the storm is rising;
- the radio is silent until the rim, where Castle's voice comes back.

You watch the crew land at your base. It always felt like it could be one-way, and the game makes you believe it, but you were prepared. **There is one ending.**

- **`docs/traversal.md`:**
  - phase 2's ground kinds gain ripple belts, boulder fields, platy lava, frost and hollow ground;
  - phase 3 gains the drone's markers;
  - phase 5's ropes also go down skylights and across fissures.
- **The rover** (`scripts/crew_rover.gd`): about 3 m/s; bogging, wheel wear and sleeping aboard.
- **The radio:** radio shadow and relay beacons.
- **Events:** solar storms, dust storms, marsquakes and fresh impacts.
