# The interference: options

**Status: options for the user to choose, 7 Nov.** Nothing here is built. Once one is picked it gets planted in the arrival story (master-design §2) and the story threads (§7), and built later.

**The idea (the user, 7 Nov):** the storm alone didn't break everything. Something larger caused interference that sent the walkers' navigation wrong and explains other faults, and finding it is a huge late discovery, core to the story.

**What it has to do:**
- explain why the walkers stalled in the wrong places, why the pallets were strewn, and why the base's landing beacon went silent;
- leave small clues from the first sol, so the reveal feels earned, not bolted on;
- end in something big near the end of the game, without stealing the ancient-life find (master-design §7, the wonder thread);
- tie into what's already decided: the walkers, the map's 25 places, the outdoor instruments, the voices, the storm's return and both endings.

Three options follow. **A leads**, as the user leaned towards "real, but unexpected".

---

## A. The buried heart (real, but unexpected) · Recommended

**What it is.** Under the Reach lies a huge buried body of once-molten rock: an old magma intrusion, cooled billions of years ago into iron-rich rock that froze Mars's ancient magnetic field into itself. It makes the Reach one of the most strongly magnetised patches of ground on the planet, far stronger than the orbiters' coarse maps showed. It's also still faintly warm deep down. Long ago its heat drove hot springs into the crater lake.

**Why it's real science.**
- Mars has no global magnetic field today, but its crust holds strong patches of old magnetism (mapped by Mars Global Surveyor).
- InSight measured a field at the surface about ten times stronger than orbit predicted, and it wavered at night.
- MAVEN found auroras that cluster over these magnetic patches. In March 2024 Perseverance saw the first visible green aurora from the ground, after a solar storm.
- Buried intrusions driving hot springs is how scientists think some Martian clays and minerals formed, and hot springs are a leading idea for where life could start.

**Why it broke everything.** Three things in the same week, and the dust storm got all the blame:
1. **The storm hid the sun and stars,** so the walkers lost their sun and star sensors and fell back on magnetic heading.
2. **The Reach's magnetism bent that heading.** Each walker drifted in a curve, some by kilometres, and stalled lost. The pallets' guided descent used the same fallback, so they came down scattered.
3. **A solar storm hit during the dust storm.** The Reach's field funnelled its particles down onto the planned site, charging and frying the landing beacon. That's why it's silent, and why your entry ran long.

**Early clues (first sols, small and deniable):**
- **The compass strip wobbles** near certain places (it's always on, so the player sees it without being told). It's a few degrees and it settles, easy to blame on the suit.
- **Walker 1's tracks curve** in a long, smooth arc instead of a straight line home. A thought in Notes: "Why didn't it walk straight?"
- **The stalled walkers point the wrong way:** each one faces somewhere other than where it was heading.
- **A faint green glow on the horizon** on a rare night: an aurora. It's beautiful and unexplained at first.
- **The orbiter crew mention radio noise** over the Reach on their drive chats (Castellanos), and joke about it.

**The trail (middle game):**
- **The walkers' nav logs.** Each walker you repair gives up its log: a headings plot that shows the drift. Lay them over the Map and they bend around one point, out past the crater.
- **A magnetometer** becomes an outdoor instrument where placement matters (like the seismometer): put it at different places, and each reading adds to a magnetic map on the slate. A new Map layer fills in as you survey.
- **The seismometer** sees odd quiet zones (sound waves slowing through warm rock).
- **The chaos blocks (place 22)** have measurable heat flow, already planned as "heat-flow ground".
- **Earth's letters change tone,** from apology about the storm to curiosity: the CAPCOMs start asking you for readings.
- **Your own thoughts follow it.** Notes go from "the suit's compass is off again" to "the walkers didn't get lost by accident".

**The late reveal (near the end):**
- The magnetic map closes into one shape: a giant buried body under the crater and the chaos blocks.
- A deep core or a radar sounding at its edge proves it's an ancient intrusion, still a little warm at depth.
- **It joins the life thread:** the hot springs it fed are where the lakebed's microbial signs come from. The interference that wrecked your landing and the life find are one story. *The thing that broke your base is the reason anything ever lived here.*
- It's a discovery about Mars itself, the kind that rewrites the planet's history, and you made it because you were knocked off course.

**How it ties in:**
- **The walkers:** their curved walks become evidence, and repairing each one gives you a log.
- **The map:** a new magnetic layer. Places 17-19 (crater, mound, delta) and 22 (chaos blocks) become the heart of it; the highland walls (25) mark its edge.
- **The storm's return:** this time you know a solar storm can come with it. You watch for the aurora as a warning, shut down sensitive kit and stay inside, rather than losing everything again. The big late test becomes one you're ready for.
- **The welcome ending:** the crew land guided by your magnetic map, safely and on target, because you worked out what went wrong. Your survey is how they get down.
- **The go-home ending:** your final report carries the discovery home.
- **The voices:** Castellanos's radio-noise jokes become the first clue; the CAPCOM letters carry Earth's growing interest; ARDEN stays in the habitat and only reports its own readings.

**What it changes in the canon:** small. §2 gains one line (the walkers fell back on magnetic heading and the Reach bent it; a solar storm silenced the beacon). §7's wonder thread gains the buried heart as its last step before the life find. "Real science only" holds.

**Build cost:** the compass wobble, curved walker tracks and walker logs are cheap. The magnetometer and the Map layer are one new instrument plus a layer. The aurora is a sky effect. The reveal is a discovery chain using parts we already have (cores, radar, the journal).

## B. Someone was here first (human-made)

**What it is.** Another lander touched down in the Reach before your cargo: a quiet, uncrewed mission by another agency or a company, never announced. Its relay transmitter failed stuck on, broadcasting on the walkers' band. It jammed their navigation and drowned out the beacon.

**Early clues:** a hum on the radio band; a strange, tidy track that isn't a walker's; Earth's letters dodging questions.

**The trail:** triangulate the noise with radio readings from different places; find bits of unfamiliar hardware; Earth's story shifts under pressure.

**The late reveal:** you find the other lander far out, broken, and its logs. They show it was sent to look for exactly what you've been finding, and that someone on Earth knew more than they told you.

**Strengths:** grounded and believable; adds human drama and a mystery about who to trust.

**Weaknesses:** the big moment is about people on Earth, not Mars; it makes Mission Control less trustworthy, which works against the warm one-way letters; and a found lander is a smaller wonder than the life find, so the two compete in a muddled way.

---

## C. Something not human

**What it is.** The interference comes from something that isn't natural and isn't ours: a buried structure or signal source.

**Strengths:** the biggest possible reveal.

**Weaknesses:** it breaks "real science only", which the whole wonder thread is built on. It would also overshadow the ancient-microbes finding: once there's an artefact, fossils of microbes feel small. It changes what kind of game this is. A softer version, left ambiguous forever, still pulls players towards theories and away from the real discoveries.

---

## Recommendation

**A, the buried heart.** It explains every fault with real physics. Its clues are things already in the game (the compass strip, the walkers' tracks, the outdoor instruments, the voices). And it doesn't compete with the life find: it *leads* to it, so the end of the game has one great payoff rather than two rival ones.

## Sources

- InSight's surface magnetometer: a field about ten times stronger than predicted, varying by day and night (Johnson et al., Nature Geoscience, 2020).
- MAVEN's discrete auroras over crustal magnetic fields, and Perseverance's visible green aurora after the March 2024 solar storm (Knutsen et al., Science Advances, 2025).
- Solar particle events blacking out radio and radar at Mars (Mars Express MARSIS and MAVEN observations).
- Hydrothermal systems and Martian clays as places life might start (Ehlmann et al., Nature, 2011).
