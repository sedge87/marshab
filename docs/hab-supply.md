# The habitat's supply, and your body (14 Nov 2026)

*Agreed with the user on 14 Nov 2026, the third design talk after `docs/pillars.md`. Where `docs/systems-and-items.md` §1 and §1.6 disagree, this wins. Times are in fractions of a sol, so they hold if the length of a sol changes. Every number is a starting point for playtest.*

## Three needs per base: power, air and heat

- Each base's core panel and the slate show **three simple readings:**
  - **power:** what comes in, what goes out, and the battery;
  - **air:** good, stale or thin;
  - **heat.**
- **A real chain runs behind them:** ice, then water, then oxygen, with scrubbers clearing the CO₂.
- **You only touch the chain by building and upgrading,** never by topping things up. For example: the panel goes amber on power, so you add an array or a battery bank before the dust season.
- There's no other routine upkeep. Wear and spares stay as they are (systems-and-items §1.9).

## When the power runs out: slow, loud and fair

**Batteries are sized to cover a normal night.** If they empty, for example because a dust storm has buried the arrays:

1. **Load shedding:** lights dim and machines shut down, with life support last.
2. **Heat falls first.** It's cold in about **a quarter of a sol**: frost on the walls, and you can see your breath.
3. **The air goes stale after about half a sol:** alarms, and a headache blur at the edges of your view.
4. **You suit up indoors.** The suit's air is your reserve. Then you fix it, or go to the lander or the rover.

**It's never sudden, and you always hear it coming.** Failures come only from your choices or from Mars, never at random. Random faults may be added later as an option.

## Outposts: the same rules, their own supply

- Each outpost's core runs the same three needs by itself, from its own arrays and batteries and its own ice and water (which is often why you built it there).
- **It keeps going while you're away.** If it fails, you hear it on the radio, and you come back to a cold, stale outpost, never a dead one.
- There are no cables or pipes between bases.

## Your body

There are four needs. Each is felt on your body, not shown as a meter.

| Need | Indoors | Outside |
|---|---|---|
| **Air and warmth** | the base's air and heat | the suit's air, heat and battery |
| **Water (thirst)** | drink at the Galley, or at the core's tap before you have a Galley | sip from the suit's drink bag, about 1 litre, refilled at the suit rack |
| **Food (hunger)** | eat at the Galley | eat a food bar from the suit, packed at the Galley |
| **Sleep (tiredness)** | a bunk: full sleep in Quarters, poor in the core | sleep in the rover on long trips |

- **The model is NASA's:** the shuttle-era spacesuit carried a drink bag of about 1 litre, and once held a food bar in the helmet.
- **Hunger, thirst and tiredness are a pull home.** Together with the suit's air and battery, they set how long a trip can be.
- **Going without for long enough costs health.** At zero you collapse, and the blackout rule still has to be designed (pillars, "still to design" #8).

## Who sent you: an international agency

- **The mission belongs to an international Mars agency.** It's public, run by several nations, and NASA-like but fictional. That makes the flight rules, the science goals and "land the crew safely" feel natural.
- **Castle and the crew in orbit are its astronauts.**
- **The name is still to choose.** Ideas so far: "the Mars Accord", "Joint Mars Office", "the Aeolis Programme".
- Whether a company is involved (for example, one that built the cargo landers and walkers under contract) is left for later.
